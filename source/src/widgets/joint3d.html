Interactive 3D: the joint distribution, and what "summing out" and "conditioning" do to it

Drag the surface to rotate it (on a phone, swipe sideways and use the tilt slider). Pick an operation below, then move the slice.

The surface is $f_{X,Y}(x,y)$. Dashed ellipses on the floor are level sets. Raise $\rho$ and they tilt: high $x$ goes with high $y$. Height is rescaled to fit, so read numbers from the panel below.

Amber slice: fix $x=x_0$ and look along $y$. The area of that curtain is $\int f_{X,Y}(x_0,y)\,dy=f_X(x_0)$, the height of the dot on the back wall. Press Sweep and the wall traces the whole marginal $f_X$: summing out $Y$ means adding up every slice.

Amber slice: fix $y=y_0$ and look along $x$. The area of that curtain is $\int f_{X,Y}(x,y_0)\,dx=f_Y(y_0)$, the height of the dot on the side wall. Sweeping traces the marginal $f_Y$.

Amber: the slice $f_{X,Y}(x_0,y)$, whose area is only $f_X(x_0)$. Dashed black: the slice divided by $f_X(x_0)$, so its area is exactly 1. That is the conditional density $f_{Y|X}(y\mid x_0)$. Gray (lower panel): the marginal of $Y$. Set $\rho=0$ and the dashed and gray curves coincide: independence.
