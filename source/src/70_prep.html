What to prioritize for Midterm I

In the order I would study them

  1. Zero covariance does not imply independence. Independence does imply zero covariance. Expect this to be tested.
  2. Be able to derive the shortcuts: $\var[X]=\E[X^2]-\mu^2$, $\cov[X,Y]=\E[XY]-\mu_X\mu_Y$, and $\var[\bar Y]=\sigma^2/n$.
  3. Know where independence is used and where it is not. It removes the cross terms in the variance of a sum. Unbiasedness of $\bar Y$ needs only linearity.
  4. Practice LIE as a weighted average of group means, and remember that $\E[Y\mid X]$ is a random variable.
  5. Keep two distinctions clean: estimator (random) vs estimate (a number), and bias vs variance.
  6. Distinguish LLN from CLT: concentration around the population mean versus the approximately normal shape of standardized sampling error.
  7. Practice inference from start to finish: choose the alternative, calculate standard error and the test statistic, find the correct p-value, and compare it with the significance level.
  8. Interpret uncertainty correctly: confidence intervals cover a fixed parameter across repeated samples; p-values assume the null is true. Know Type I and Type II errors and power.
