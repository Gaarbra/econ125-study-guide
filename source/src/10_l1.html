Lecture 1: Models and causality

Wooldridge Chapter 1, pp 1-14

What econometrics is for

  • Testing economic theories and hypotheses
  • Estimating relationships between economic variables
  • Forecasting economic variables
  • Evaluating government and business policy

The central difficulty is that most data are observational, so you need natural experiments to argue for causality. Chapter 1 also names the four data structures: cross-sectional, time series, pooled cross sections, and panel.

From theory to a statistical model

  1. Theory. Which relationship, why you expect it, and which other variables affect it.
  2. Statistical model. Measure the variables, pick a functional form, find variation that acts like an experiment, test size and significance, and check robustness.
  3. Interpret and communicate the results.

Wage equation (job training)

$$\begin{aligned} \text{wage}={}&\beta_0+\beta_1\,\text{educ}\\ &+\beta_2\,\text{exper}+\beta_3\,\text{training}+u \end{aligned}$$

wage hourly wage. educ years of formal education. exper years of workforce experience. training weeks in job training. u everything unobserved that affects wage. $\beta_3$ is the wage effect of one more week of training. The question is whether it differs from zero and is large enough to justify paying for training.

Crime equation (from Becker's utility-maximization model)

$$\begin{aligned} \text{crime}={}&\beta_0+\beta_1\,\text{wage}+\beta_2\,\text{othinc}\\ &+\beta_3\,\text{freqarr}+\beta_4\,\text{freqconv}\\ &+\beta_5\,\text{avgsen}+\beta_6\,\text{age}+u \end{aligned}$$

crime a measure of criminal activity. wage wage from legal employment. othinc other income. freqarr frequency of prior arrests. freqconv frequency of conviction. avgsen average sentence length after conviction.

The term $u$ collects the unobserved factors. You cannot see it, so you have to make assumptions about it. Those assumptions become the regression assumptions later in the course.

Prediction vs causation

PredictionCausal effect
QuestionGiven $x$, what is the best guess for $y$?If $x$ changes for person $i$ and everything else is held fixed, how does $y$ change?
IdeaHow $\E[y\mid x]$ varies with $x$Change $x_i$ to $x_i'$, ceteris paribus
  • Individual treatment effect: one person gets an extra year of education. How does their income change?
  • Average treatment effect: a random person (or everyone) gets an extra year. How does average income change?

Causal effects are identified when predictive relationships are tested with a controlled or natural experiment that enforces ceteris paribus, at least on average.

The experimental ideal and its three threats

Randomly assign the treatment so it is independent of everything else that affects the outcome. Fertilizer and crop yield is the clean case: randomize amounts across one-acre plots and land quality and rainfall are held fixed on average.

ThreatWhat goes wrongSlide example
Omitted variablesRelevant factors you do not control for get misattributed to the treatmentLand quality and rainfall in the fertilizer study
SelectionThe people you observe differ from the populationStudents who seek tutoring are highly motivated; observing only public school students in one district when studying returns to education
SimultaneityTreatment and outcome are determined togetherPolice levels and crime; supply and demand determining prices
