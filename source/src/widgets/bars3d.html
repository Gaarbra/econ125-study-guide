Interactive 3D: your degree and wage table as bars

Each bar is one cell of the joint table: bar height is the probability $f_{X,Y}(x,y)$. Rotate it, then choose what to do with the bars.

$\lambda$ slides the joint distribution from the original ($\lambda=0$) toward the independent one $f_X(x)f_Y(y)$ ($\lambda=1$). The marginals never change.

Six bars, and they add to 1. Blue is $X=0$ (no degree), red is $X=1$ (degree).

Sum out $Y$: stack each row's three bars into one tall bar on the back wall. That stack is the marginal $f_X(x)=\sum_y f_{X,Y}(x,y)$. Lighter shades are lower wages.

Sum out $X$: stack the blue and red bars in each wage column on the side wall. That stack is the marginal $f_Y(y)=\sum_x f_{X,Y}(x,y)$.

Condition on $X$: keep only the chosen row (others fade). On the wall, each bar is divided by the row total $f_X(x)$, so the three wall bars add to 1: $f_{Y|X}(y\mid x)=f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$.
